Create a modern, elegant, responsive educational website about cervical cancer pathophysiology for women aged 30–40.
The website should feel calm, professional, feminine, and easy to understand, using a color palette inspired by the reference image: deep magenta, plum, soft pink, lilac, white, and warm beige accents. Use rounded cards, soft shadows, clean typography, and a polished medical infographic style.

The website should be a single-page scrollable layout with a top navigation bar and clickable section anchors so users can jump to sections. Include interactive-looking cards, collapsible information panels, and infographic-style visuals to simplify complex science.

Website goals

Explain the pathophysiology of cervical cancer in clear, simple language for a non-scientific audience.
Focus on:

what the cervix is and what healthy cervical cells do
HPV as the main cause
how HPV changes cell behavior
how abnormal cells develop into dysplasia and cancer
why symptoms happen
why this disease matters for women aged 30–40
Layout

1. Hero section
Large title: “HPV & Cervical Cancer”
Subtitle: a short plain-language explanation of what the site teaches.
Add a strong hero illustration of the cervix and HPV.
Include a “Start Learning” button and small navigation chips.

2. Section: What is Cervical Cancer?
Use a card with a short definition in plain language.
Add 2–3 bullet points and a cervix diagram.

3. Section: What is HPV?
Use a two-column layout.
Left side: simple explanation of HPV.
Right side: infographic or virus illustration.
Include a clickable “Learn more” card or accordion.

4. Section: How HPV changes cervical cells
Show a visual flow diagram:
Normal cervical cell → HPV infection → E6/E7 proteins → loss of cell control → abnormal growth.
Make this section highly visual and easy to follow.

5. Section: From dysplasia to cervical cancer
Create a horizontal timeline or stepped progression:
Healthy cervix → low-grade changes → high-grade changes → invasive cancer.
Use layered cell illustrations or a progression chart.

6. Section: Why it matters for women aged 30–40
Use four small cards with icons.
Explain that early stages can be silent, changes happen slowly, HPV may have been present earlier, and understanding the disease helps people recognize body changes.

7. Section: Common symptoms explained by the disease process
Use symptom cards with small illustrations:

unusual vaginal bleeding
unusual discharge
pelvic pain
pain when urinating
Each card should include a short explanation linking the symptom to tissue damage or tumor growth.

8. Summary section
End with a short recap and a calm closing message.
Add a final visual that feels hopeful and professional.
